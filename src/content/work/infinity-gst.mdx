Most businesses don't struggle with GST. They struggle with disconnected workflows.

Quotations live in one tool. Invoices in another. Expenses in a spreadsheet. GST gets calculated at month-end. So I built **LedgerFlow** — a single system that connects the entire financial lifecycle, from the first quotation sent to the final return filed — for a real business, **Infinity Enterprises**, running two brands under one registration.

## The problem

Running the business meant moving the same data across half a dozen tools:

- Creating quotations by hand
- Converting them into invoices
- Tracking payments separately
- Recording expenses somewhere else
- Calculating GST at month-end
- Preparing returns in spreadsheets

Every step duplicated information. Every month required reconciliation. The business had data everywhere — and no single source of truth.

---

## The system

One data model. Every document downstream is generated from it — a quotation becomes a proforma, a proforma becomes a payment receipt, a receipt becomes a tax invoice, and every rupee rolls up into cash flow and GST automatically.

## Small decisions that matter

The difference between a CRUD app and a real product lives in details no one asks for:

- **Invoice numbering** follows a strict `INF-YYYY-XXXX` format — auto-incremented, but manually overridable to fill a gap.
- **Amount-to-words** in Indian numbering (lakhs, crores) on every PDF, because a legal invoice requires it.
- **Bill evidence** is scoped per-record in private storage with row-level security — not a shared dumping ground.
- **No public signup.** Accounts are provisioned manually with `owner` / `ceo` roles. This is internal financial infrastructure, not a consumer app.

## Building financial infrastructure

**In financial software, correctness is the entire product.** A CRM can ship with rough edges; a GST app cannot mis-split a single tax line. Encoding the place-of-supply rules into the data model — rather than trusting a user to pick the right tax — was the decision that made everything downstream trustworthy. The schema enforces the law, not the user.

**The product is the connection, not the features.** Plenty of tools generate invoices. The value here was that a quotation *becomes* an invoice *becomes* a GST return without anyone re-entering data. Designing one record that flows through the whole business is harder than building ten isolated screens — and it's the only thing that removes reconciliation.

**Compliance is a UX problem, not just a legal one.** The real win wasn't *computing* GST — it was making month-end feel like exporting a file instead of dreading a deadline. All the hard engineering exists to make the human's job boring: the difficult part should be invisible, and the stressful part should disappear.
